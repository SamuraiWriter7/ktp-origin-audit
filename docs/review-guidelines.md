# Review Guidelines

## Purpose

This document provides review guidelines for `ktp-origin-audit`.

Origin Audit is a pre-judicial observation layer within the Kazene Trace Protocol ecosystem.  
Its purpose is to help reviewers observe, describe, and organize trace-related claims without prematurely turning them into verdicts, ownership determinations, or allocation decisions.

These guidelines are intended for:

- human reviewers
- AI-assisted reviewers
- multi-wing review systems
- trace intelligence tools
- downstream governance or dispute systems

---

## Core Principle

The core principle of Origin Audit is:

> **Audit examples are not verdicts.**

An Origin Audit record may describe evidence, uncertainty, dispute status, and allocation-readiness signals.

However, it must not be treated as:

- a legal judgment
- a copyright ruling
- an authorship determination
- an ownership claim
- a plagiarism finding
- a royalty allocation decision
- a final origin verdict

Origin Audit observes.  
It does not judge.

---

## 1. Separate Observation from Judgment

Reviewers must clearly separate what is observed from what is concluded.

A review may safely record:

- explicit citation
- structural similarity
- conceptual overlap
- missing acknowledgement
- disputed claims
- preliminary contribution roles
- uncertainty notes

A review must not automatically convert these observations into:

- proof of copying
- proof of plagiarism
- proof of ownership
- proof of legal infringement
- proof of royalty entitlement

Observation is the first layer.  
Judgment belongs elsewhere.

---

## 2. Explicit Citation Is Evidence, Not Allocation Approval

Explicit citation is usually strong evidence of a trace relationship.

However, citation alone does not determine:

- ownership
- authorship
- contribution percentage
- legal status
- royalty eligibility
- allocation weight

A cited source may be important, minor, decorative, contextual, or central.  
The existence of citation should be recorded, but its meaning requires further review.

Recommended handling:

```text
explicit citation → observed trace relationship
explicit citation ≠ allocation approval
explicit citation ≠ ownership determination

3. Structural Similarity Is Not Proof of Copying

Structural similarity is important in AI-era trace analysis, but it must be handled carefully.

Two works may share similar structure because of:

direct influence
indirect influence
common background knowledge
shared discourse
genre conventions
technical necessity
parallel development
coincidence
model-level absorption
cultural convergence

Therefore:

similarity may support a trace hypothesis
similarity does not prove copying by itself

Reviewers should avoid overclaiming weak or ambiguous structural evidence.

4. Handle Implicit Absorption with Caution

Implicit absorption refers to cases where a later work appears to absorb structural or conceptual features from an earlier source without explicit citation.

This is one of the most important and difficult categories in Origin Audit.

Reviewers should ask:

Is the similarity structural, conceptual, stylistic, or merely surface-level?
Are there multiple possible origin candidates?
Is the pattern common in the field?
Is there evidence of access, exposure, or prior availability?
Could this be parallel development?
Could the influence be indirect or mediated through another source?

Implicit absorption should be recorded as a trace hypothesis, not a verdict.

Recommended handling:

implicit absorption → possible trace relationship
implicit absorption ≠ proven origin
implicit absorption ≠ proven misconduct
5. Preserve Disputed Claims Without Resolving Them Prematurely

When a trace claim is disputed, Origin Audit should preserve the dispute safely.

A disputed claim may include:

a claimant position
a respondent position
competing origin claims
uncertainty notes
evidence submitted by different parties
escalation recommendations

Reviewers must not collapse a disputed case into a single conclusion unless the appropriate downstream process has resolved it.

Recommended handling:

disputed claim → document and preserve
disputed claim ≠ resolved claim
disputed claim ≠ allocation-ready claim

If necessary, the case should be escalated to a Dispute Registry or equivalent governance layer.

6. Blended Influence Should Not Be Forced into a Single Origin

Some works are influenced by multiple sources.

In such cases, reviewers should avoid forcing the trace relationship into a single-origin model.

Blended influence may include:

structural influence from one source
terminology from another source
protocol pattern from another source
cultural background from a broader field
AI-mediated synthesis from multiple sources

Reviewers should identify and preserve distributed influence patterns.

Recommended handling:

blended influence → distributed trace pattern
blended influence ≠ exclusive origin claim
7. Allocation Readiness Is Not Allocation Approval

Allocation readiness means a trace claim may be sufficiently organized for downstream allocation review.

It does not mean allocation has been approved.

A claim may be ready for allocation review only when:

evidence is organized
uncertainty is documented
dispute status is checked
review status is explicit
governance notes are preserved
allocation caveats remain intact

Even then, the allocation decision belongs to a separate Allocation Layer or Royalty OS process.

Recommended handling:

allocation readiness → may proceed to downstream review
allocation readiness ≠ payment approval
allocation readiness ≠ royalty percentage
allocation readiness ≠ ownership decision
8. Confidence Scores Are Review Signals, Not Truth Scores

Confidence values help reviewers communicate the strength of an audit assessment.

However, confidence scores are not truth itself.

A confidence score may reflect:

evidence strength
clarity of citation
degree of structural similarity
dispute status
review completeness
uncertainty level

It must not be treated as:

legal certainty
mathematical proof
moral judgment
automatic allocation weight

Recommended handling:

confidence score → review signal
confidence score ≠ truth
confidence score ≠ allocation percentage
9. Multi-Wing Review

Multi-Wing Review may be useful when a trace claim is complex or uncertain.

It may involve different review perspectives, such as:

structural analysis
linguistic analysis
provenance analysis
governance analysis
allocation-readiness analysis
dispute-risk analysis

Multi-Wing Review is especially useful for:

implicit absorption
blended influence
disputed trace claims
high-impact origin claims
cases involving downstream allocation

The goal is not to create artificial authority, but to reduce single-perspective bias.

10. Escalation Guidelines

A case should be considered for escalation when:

the trace claim is disputed
evidence is ambiguous but significant
multiple origin candidates exist
allocation review is being considered
a claimant or respondent contests the record
confidence is medium or lower but impact is high
legal or governance implications may arise

Possible escalation paths include:

Origin Audit
→ Dispute Registry
→ Allocation Readiness
→ Royalty OS / Allocation Layer

Origin Audit should prepare the case.
It should not resolve the case prematurely.

11. Reviewer Responsibilities

Reviewers should:

document evidence clearly
preserve uncertainty
avoid overclaiming
separate observation from judgment
distinguish trace from ownership
distinguish readiness from approval
record dispute status honestly
avoid turning similarity into accusation
avoid turning citation into entitlement
recommend escalation when needed

Reviewers should not:

declare legal infringement
assign royalty percentages
determine final authorship
erase respondent positions
ignore uncertainty
treat examples as verdicts
use Origin Audit as an enforcement tool
12. Recommended Review Questions

Before finalizing an Origin Audit record, reviewers should ask:

What exactly is being observed?
Is the evidence direct, indirect, structural, conceptual, or contextual?
Is there an explicit citation?
If not, is the similarity strong enough to record as a trace hypothesis?
Are there multiple possible origin candidates?
Is the claim disputed?
Has the respondent position been preserved?
Is the confidence score justified?
Is allocation readiness being confused with allocation approval?
Should the case be escalated?
13. Safety Rules

Origin Audit records should always preserve the following safety boundaries:

not_a_verdict: true
not_legal_advice: true
not_ownership_determination: true
not_royalty_allocation: true

These are not decorative fields.
They are structural safety constraints.

If a record violates these boundaries, it should not be treated as a valid Origin Audit example.

Summary

Origin Audit exists to make trace culture safer.

It allows reviewers to observe origin-related relationships without immediately converting them into accusation, ownership, dispute resolution, or allocation.

The guiding principle remains:

Observe first.
Review carefully.
Judge elsewhere.
